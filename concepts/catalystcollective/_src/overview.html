The overview · an evolving model for EO Nashville's strategic council

A membership, not a waiting room.

Catalyst, as we know it, is the accelerator. The Collective is the community you apply to join after graduating from the Catalyst coursework. Catalyst Collective is an invitation-based, ongoing membership with continual access to an entrepreneurial community and the resources to help you, whatever your growth targets and strategy. It is the evolution of Bridge, not a rename.

{{sh:door-open|The concept|Three rooms, one community}}

Catalyst already exists. EO Nashville already exists. Today the room in the middle is Bridge: a forum, a mentor, and invitations to learning events for $1,750 a year, which the people in it describe as a holding pattern. The Collective is not Bridge with more bullets. It is the evolution of Bridge: an ongoing membership, by invitation, with tracks, programming and a community of its own.

{{sh:note-pencil|The concept, updated October 7|How you get in: apply, then the committee invites}}

The Collective is an application, not an automatic next step. Graduates of Catalyst may apply to become members of the Collective, and past alumni may apply at any point as well. Applicants are invited on the same kinds of factors EO weighs on an application, with Catalyst's revenue thresholds, tiered by industry, in place of EO's line.

  1. {{icon:graduation-cap}}Step 1Finish CatalystGraduate the Catalyst coursework: thirteen classes, the cohort, the mentor.
  2. {{icon:note-pencil}}Step 2ApplyNew graduates apply when they finish. Past alumni can apply at any time; there is no expiration.
  3. {{icon:users-four}}Step 3Committee reviewThe Collective's own committee reviews the application, informed by the graduate's moderator and mentor.
  4. {{icon:envelope-open}}Step 4Invited and placedAn invitation, then a short intake places the new member on a track and into a forum of matched peers.

What the committee looks for

  • {{icon:identification-badge}}The same kinds of factors as an EO application: founder or controlling owner, committed to the business, a fit with EO's culture and values.
  • {{icon:chart-bar}}Catalyst's revenue thresholds, tiered by industry, in place of EO's single line. The tiers are set by the committee.
  • {{icon:shield-check}}Not in crisis firefighting mode: the business runs, even if it is small.
  • {{icon:users-three}}Staff or a team of support, so everything does not rely on the owner: employees, 1099s, freelancers, agents, bots, a bookkeeper, whatever the mix is.
  • {{icon:target}}Truly committed to the business.
  • {{icon:brain}}A learning mindset: a thirst for learning and the aptitude to apply it to stabilize and scale the business.

The invitation is a lever, not a wall: in a strong class it might be everybody. Applicants not invited yet are best suited to repeat Catalyst. Colton's proposal is a discounted repeat; today a repeat costs the full $3,500. See the third journey.

{{sh:presentation-chart|The September 17 council, kept straight|What was presented, where the room leaned, what is open}}

Ninety minutes, sole topic, at Espaces Burton Hills. Three different things came out of that room and they are kept apart here on purpose. None of it is minutes and none of it was a vote. If a line is wrong, say so at the bottom of the page.

{{icon:lightbulb}}The concept

What Colton presented

  • {{icon:tag}}Not Bridge, and not Bridge renamed. EO Global already has a product called Bridge and it confuses everyone, and the point is bigger than the name. Working name: Catalyst Collective, an ongoing membership, by invitation. It is the evolution of Bridge, not a rename.
  • {{icon:users-three}}A membership, not a waiting room, between Catalyst and EO, with programming of its own. It holds two kinds of members on purpose: those scaling toward EO, and those who are profitable, happy, and not chasing bigger.
  • {{icon:envelope-open}}By invitation. A committee, informed by moderators and mentors, extends it. A learning mindset and culture fit are the gate. Updated by Colton on October 7: getting in is an application. Graduates apply, past alumni can apply at any time, and applicants are invited on EO-style factors with Catalyst's revenue thresholds tiered by industry.
  • {{icon:signpost}}Placement into a track by a short intake, then a forum of matched peers, mentorship, and a monthly learning calendar with content for each track, led mostly by EO members who have real content to give. EO House is the room.
  • {{icon:identification-badge}}Its own committee with a chair, plus a dedicated resource for logistics, events, invitations and volunteers. The financial model prices that person at $75,000.
  • {{icon:money}}A working price of $2,500 to $3,000 a year, against $1,750 for Bridge today.
  • {{icon:rocket-launch}}Members graduate into EO when they qualify. "Once you qualify for EO, congratulations, you're now part of EO."
{{icon:ear}}Where the room leaned

Colton's read of the recording

  • The middle room as it stands today is not working, and it needs a name that is not Bridge.
  • Lay the whole path out on day one of Catalyst, through the moderators, so the next room is never a surprise.
  • Two forum tracks rather than three, with three content buckets on one learning calendar. Several members also saw the buckets as a useful filter for all EO programming.
  • Build the programming from EO members and existing events first, rather than writing a new curriculum.
  • It needs someone to run it, and it needs a financial model before anything else. Named in the room: a staff position and a committee of about six with a Collective chair; David Frazier for the math. No one was named to run it yet.
  • Catalyst's expected loss this year ($10,000 to $12,000) is acceptable as a recruiting cost.
{{icon:question}}Still open

Not settled by anyone

  • A separate membership, or simply continuing in Catalyst? Both were said in the room.
  • Price, and initiation credit. $2,500 and $3,000 were both said. One member suggested a year of Collective dues could count toward EO initiation. Colton is torn ("full price or free").
  • The number. Colton's slide said keep 50% of each graduating class; his target here is 75%. Matt has asked for a clear membership goal.
  • The programming. Sameera's word was "hazy": re-bucketed EO events plus think tanks, or something built? Audit a few classes, or adopt an operating system like EOS or Petra?
  • The revenue tiers by industry. Who sets them, and at what numbers.
  • Applicants who are not invited. Repeat Catalyst? At a discount (Colton's proposal, not discussed in the room)? A lighter bootcamp?
  • A carrot for EO. One member's caution: the Collective should never get so good that qualifying members stay in it instead of joining EO. A time limit was asked about; Colton's answer was graduating out on qualifying.
  • Is the Bridge stigma real? Michael Burcham's theory, unverified. The alumni survey asks.
  • Member commitment across committees and boards: seat-cost transparency and five-bullet expectation documents were floated. Colton is soliciting ideas.

{{icon:user-circle}}Perspective: Brittany Lorenzi

EO Catalyst: Bridging the Gap to $1M

Emailed September 17 with #Calvin. A brainstorm she wrote before the meeting, drawing on about a dozen Catalyst participants she has mentored.

Graduates fall into three populations ($1M Ready, Growth Gap, Foundation Gap). A Stage 2 that is earned, not automatic. For the Growth Gap group, implementation and accountability instead of more curriculum: a Strategic Growth Plan, rotating advisors by function, implementation labs, and 90-day execution cycles.

Where it touches the concept: it overlaps on earning the next room and on diagnosing before placing. It is written for graduates heading toward $1M and EO, and does not address members who choose to stay small, which the concept does.

{{icon:user-circle}}Perspective: Jay Graves

Project Elevate Nashville

Emailed September 17 with #Calvin. A working draft from February 2026 that Jay had discussed with Colton and Richard. Council material.

The whole journey as four tiers: Catalyst; Ascent Nashville as the middle membership (Catalyst graduates, revenue up to $2M, Ascent-only forums, all Nashville events, $1,750 a year with scholarships); EO Nashville with the revenue minimum raised to $2M in 2027 and current members grandfathered; and Summit for 15-year members.

Where it touches the concept: the same middle room, at a different price and with different event access, and tied to the threshold question, which Colton set aside for now.
