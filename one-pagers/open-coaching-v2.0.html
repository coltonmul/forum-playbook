Open Coaching

Open coaching unlocks the depth of an unplanned presentation and sets up better experience shares. It is an EQ tool: a short run of curious questions at the front of a deep dive, never a fact-finding interview.

First, Confirm It Is an EQ Topic [open coaching is wasted on IQ topics]

EQ · Coach It
  • The whys. Significant, unresolved, emotionally complex: fears, dreams, doubts, the conversation they are nervous to have.
  • The things you cannot take to Claude or ChatGPT and get answers on.
  • Earns the 30-40 minute deep dive: coaching, two minutes of silence, then experience shares.
IQ · Route It
  • The what and the how. Process, details, facts and figures: comp plans, sales funnels, what everybody is doing with AI.
  • Gets 10-12 minutes as a lightning round, 60 seconds each around the room.
  • Not open coaching material, and never the deep dive slot.

The Process [7-8 minutes, questions only, no advice]

  1. Orient the presenter. Three questions set the frame: "What is a single headline for the topic and the tension?" "What are 3 emotions related to the topic?" "What types of experiences are you curious to hear from the group?"
  2. Get the broad strokes. Short, factual questions: who are the key players, is there a decision to be made or an action to be taken, has a situation like this happened before?
  3. Go where the feelings are. Ask how the topic relates to insecurities, identity, relationships, personal values, trust. Stay curious and ask the difficult question.
  4. Guard the lane. It is not the coach's job to console or to solve. Do not sweat transitions, and do not be afraid to interrupt a rabbit hole that is not pertinent.

Close: Define the Experience Share Request [every request starts "A time when you felt..."]

  1. Near-exact. Same situation, same feelings. "A time when you felt undermined by a business partner's lack of leadership."
  2. Same emotions, different situation. "A time when you felt that same tension confronting someone on an awkward topic."
  3. The other side. A different seat in the same situation. "A time when you felt exposed because someone confronted you about a weakness of yours."

"Resonance, not relevance. An experience share is whatever resonated with you, not an identical situation."

Colton Mulligan, forum trainings, May-June 2026
