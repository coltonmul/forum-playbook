Forum Masterclass

One afternoon that makes every forum in the chapter better. Colton Mulligan teaches the tools and best practices of forum to the whole chapter, then sits members at mixed tables with people from other forums to use those tools on something real. It fits inside a chapter retreat, an annual meeting, or its own day.

Three parts, one arc

The first part gets the moderators ready. The second teaches everyone the same tools. The third puts those tools to work with people members do not usually sit with.

01 · Optional

The night before

Mon / 2026-11-16 / 8:10 AM CT · dinner or happy hour with the moderators

A dinner or happy hour with the chapter's forum moderators, moderators-elect, the forum chair and the members who care most about forum. It is a good night on its own, and it makes the next day work: these are the people who moderate the tables in part three.

  • Walk the exercise. Each moderator sees the worksheet and the timing for the connection exercise, and runs a minute of it at the table.
  • Hold the clock. How to keep every member to their time, and what to say when someone runs long.
  • Keep it experience, not advice. How to catch a question that is really advice, and how to steer a dominant voice back to the group.
  • Shape the masterclass. What the moderators say their forums are stuck on goes into the next day's session.

Ten to twenty-five people is typical. The chapter picks the place; a private room at a restaurant works well.

03 · Chapter-wide, in mixed tables

The connection exercise

Mon / 2026-11-16 / 8:10 AM CT · the tools from part two, used for real

Members leave their own forum for an hour and a half and sit at a table of five or six, each from a different forum. A moderator from the night before runs each table. The rules are the ones they just learned: confidentiality, the clock, questions instead of advice, and the 5% as the target.

The line Colton hears most afterward: "I did not know forum could be like that." Members take that back to their own forum.

How many tables does your chapter need?

10tables of 5 or 6
10moderators, one per table

Tables of four lose energy, and seven or more run long. Five or six works.

Two exercises chapters have run

EO Philadelphia, November 2026 · the chapter's theme was connection

Each member picks three people in their life, personal, family or work, and writes who it is, what they will do, and why the relationship matters. Two minutes per person at the table. Nobody asks questions and nobody gives advice; the moderator keeps the time.

One to repairA connection that broke or faded. Apologize, have the conversation you have been avoiding, or reach out first.
One to invest inAlready good, and the one that matters most. One real conversation, a trip together, or mentoring your number two.
One to set boundaries withEnd it, or cut off the part that is not working. Say no to one thing, or limit the time you give it.

It ends with a parking lot: what each member is taking back to their own forum as a topic or a goal for next year.

EO Southeast Virginia, Forum Fusion, April 2025 · the forum mashup agenda

  1. 0:00A one-word open from everyone.
  2. 0:01Two-minute introductions: name, business, family, then "If you really knew me, you would know something in my 5% is..."
  3. 0:16Two more one-minute rounds: why the thing I shared is in my 5%.
  4. 0:36Ten minutes of writing on three conversations: the most important one someone had with me, the most important one I had with someone else, and the one I still need to have.
  5. 0:47Ten minutes each to share. Any time left goes to open-ended questions only, out of curiosity, with no problem solving.
  6. 1:58A one-minute takeaway from everyone.

Then everyone comes back to the big room for takeaways and commitments: thirty seconds each, fifteen to twenty voices, and a clear finish.
